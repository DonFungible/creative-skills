# AI production savings: documented case studies (film, TV, animation, VFX, advertising, music)

Research date: 2026-11-11. Scope: productions and studios that have *documented* cutting time or cost with AI, with before/after numbers wherever they exist.

**Evidence labels used throughout**

- **Verified/documented figure**: a number published by the company itself in a filing, shareholder letter or official release.
- **Executive claim**: a named person said it (including on an earnings call). Nobody has independently checked it.
- **Vendor claim**: a case study from the AI tool maker. This is marketing.
- **Estimate/projection**: a forecast or model, not an actual outcome.

Earnings-call remarks are labelled **Executive claim**, not "verified", because they are not audited line items. Statements in a shareholder letter are labelled **Verified/documented** only to mean that the company formally published them. They are still self-reported.

## 1. Summary

- **Netflix gives the best-sourced numbers from a major studio, and they are still self-reported.** Ted Sarandos said on the Q2 2025 call that the building-collapse shot in *El Eternauta* was "completed 10 times faster" than with traditional VFX, and that it would otherwise not have been feasible on that show's budget ([transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/07/17/netflix-nflx-q2-2025-earnings-call-transcript)). On the Q2 2026 call he said 17 minutes of AI-enhanced footage in *The American Experiment* were made "twice as fast and at half the cost" ([Fortune, 2026-07-16](https://fortune.com/2026/07/16/netflix-made-17-minutes-of-a-documentary-twice-as-fast-and-at-half-the-cost-amid-a-20-billion-projected-cost-for-content/)). The Q2 2026 letter says GenAI workflows were used on "roughly 300" titles in 2026, mostly in post-production ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2217157/netflix-says-it-s-already-used-ai-in-roughly-300-titles-this-year)). Netflix gives no baseline costs and no per-title figures.
- **Brand marketing produced the clearest dollar figures.** Klarna said genAI image tools cut image production costs by about $6M a year and its image development cycle from 6 weeks to 7 days ([Reuters via The Star, 2024-05-28](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2024/05/28/klarna-using-genai-to-cut-marketing-costs-by-10-million-annually); [Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/klarna-cuts-marketing-agency-spend-by-25-aided-by-ai/496305)). Unilever said product imagery made with digital twins and AI is "two times faster, 50% cheaper" ([Unilever press release, 2025-03](https://www.unilever.com/news/press-and-media/press-releases/2025/unilever-reinvents-product-shoots-with-digital-twins-and-ai)). Mondelez is targeting a 30–50% cut in content production cost ([Reuters via KSL, 2025-10](https://www.ksl.com/article/51394930/oreo-maker-mondelez-to-use-new-generative-ai-tool-to-slash-marketing-costs)).
- **Coca-Cola's AI holiday ads (2024, 2025) show how time and cost can diverge.** The company reports production fell from about a year to about a month, but the 2025 spot still involved about 100 people and more than 70,000 generated clips ([WSJ via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2580421544)). Coca-Cola never disclosed a budget, and both years drew public backlash.
- **Single-shot and single-sequence wins are the most credible film/TV pattern.** Examples are *El Eternauta*; the Goliath origin sequence in *House of David* ("a couple weeks" versus "four or five months", per Runway's vendor case study); and the R-to-PG-13 dialogue fix on *Fall* (about 2 weeks of "neural reshoots" versus reshoots costing "millions" and taking months, per the director). Every one of these figures is self-reported by someone with a commercial stake.
- **Localization and dubbing have the most consistent task-level numbers, and nearly all are vendor case studies.** Deepdub reports 60% faster turnaround and 40% lower cost on 30 episodes of *Follow the Money* for Topic ([Deepdub](https://deepdub.ai/case-study/topic)). ElevenLabs reports Gaia saving 25% on time and 10% on cost ([ElevenLabs, 2025-04-28](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/gaia)).
- **"AI-native studio" cost claims of 70–90% are unaudited pitches.** Examples: Toonstar ("80% faster and 90% cheaper"), Utopai ("70%+ lower production cost"), Staircase (features under $500K), *Critterz* (under $30M, about 9 months, versus $100–200M and about 3 years). As of November 2026 I found no released feature that confirms these economics with actual costs. *Critterz* reached the Cannes 2026 **market** with footage via AGC Studios. I found no confirmation that it premiered at the festival ([Deadline, 2026-05-04](https://deadline.com/2026/05/open-ai-produced-animated-family-film-critterz-cannes-1236879586/)).
- **Some high-profile partnerships under-delivered.** The Lionsgate–Runway custom-model deal hit "early snags": the catalog was too small to train a capable model, and rights questions arose ([TheWrap, 2025-09-22](https://www.thewrap.com/lionsgate-runway-ai-deal-ip-model-concerns)). In June 2026 the deal was restructured into an equity stake plus joint development ([Runway](https://runway.com/news/runway-and-lionsgate-expand-partnership)).
- **The only "measured" task-level numbers come from consultancies and studio tests, not controlled studies.** Roland Berger models AI rotoscoping savings of 20–65% of person-days depending on genre ([Roland Berger, 2025-02-26](https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/AI-Innovations-for-VFX-and-animation.html)). In early tests LAIKA saw "a 50% time savings for rotoscoping tasks" ([Digital Engineering, 2020-08-25](https://www.digitalengineering247.com/article/sigggraph-2020-a-peek-behind-laikas-stop-motion-magic/news)). I found no peer-reviewed controlled study of time savings in VFX or animation.
- **In music, the documented value is mostly about feasibility, not savings.** Examples are the Beatles' "Now and Then" (MAL audio separation rescued an unusable demo) and Randy Travis's "Where That Came From" (an AI voice model built from his catalog). Most of the rest is usage statistics. Deezer reports that more than 50% of new uploads are AI-generated but only 1–3% of streams are, with up to 70–85% of those streams flagged as fraudulent ([Deezer, 2026-07](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/)). The label settlements with Udio and Suno are licensing deals, not production-savings evidence.
- **Overall quality of evidence: weak to moderate.** Almost every number is self-reported by a party with an interest: an executive talking to investors, a vendor selling tools, or a startup raising money. Baselines ("what it would have cost") are almost never shown. No independent audit of any case was found. The direction is consistent (big speed-ups on narrowly scoped shots, images and localization tasks), but the magnitudes should be treated as upper bounds.

## 3. Case notes

### Film and TV

#### Netflix: *El Eternauta* (The Eternaut), 2025
- **Claim:** On the Q2 2025 earnings call, Sarandos said "that VFX sequence was completed 10 times faster" than with traditional VFX tools, and that "the cost of it would just not have been feasible for a show in that budget" ([Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/07/17/netflix-nflx-q2-2025-earnings-call-transcript); official PDF [here](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/2025/q2/Netflix-Inc-_Earnings-Call_Transcript.pdf)). Netflix described it as the first GenAI final footage to appear on screen in one of its originals ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/streaming/netflix-is-already-using-generative-ai-in-its-original-shows-201209502.html)).
- **Evidence:** Executive claim. It covers one shot, with no baseline hours or dollars given.
- **Caveat:** "10x" applies to a single sequence, not the series. Sarandos framed AI as making shows "better, not just cheaper".

#### Netflix: 2025–2026 shareholder letters and *The American Experiment*
- **Q3 2025 letter (2025-10-21):** *Happy Gilmore 2* used GenAI, ML and Eyeline volumetric capture for de-aging in a flashback. *Billionaires' Bunker* used GenAI in pre-production for previs of wardrobe and sets ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2025/10/22/netflix-q3-earnings-miss-best-ad-sales-quarter-ever); [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/business/netflix-q3-report-animation-ai-255980.html)). These are usage disclosures with no savings figures.
- **Q2 2026 letter (2026-07-16):** "In 2026, GenAI workflows have been used in roughly 300 of our titles", with "the largest concentration of work in post-production". It also says "we are increasingly leveraging these tools to deliver higher quality output more quickly and at a lower cost", and that some productions "would have had to leave out key shots and sequences" without GenAI ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2217157/netflix-says-it-s-already-used-ai-in-roughly-300-titles-this-year); [IBC](https://www.ibc.org/artificial-intelligence/news/netflix-uses-genai-on-300-titles-in-2026/22794); letter PDF: [Netflix IR](https://s22.q4cdn.com/959853165/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/FINAL-Q2-26-Shareholder-Letter.pdf)). I could not text-extract the PDF myself, so the quotes come via Engadget and IBC.
- **Q2 2026 call:** Sarandos said the 17 minutes of AI-enhanced footage in *The American Experiment* were "produced twice as fast and at half the cost of previous options". He also said savings "will likely be reinvested into more content" ([Fortune, 2026-07-16](https://fortune.com/2026/07/16/netflix-made-17-minutes-of-a-documentary-twice-as-fast-and-at-half-the-cost-amid-a-20-billion-projected-cost-for-content/)). Netflix had bought Ben Affleck's AI company InterPositive in March 2026, and Sarandos called results from it "early days".
- **Caveat:** "Used in 300 titles" says nothing about depth of use. Commentators noted the lack of title-level outcome data ([letsdatascience summary](https://letsdatascience.com/news/netflix-says-genai-workflows-touched-roughly-300-titles-so-f-0891e2c3)).

#### Amazon: *House of David* (Wonder Project, Prime Video)
- **Season 1 (2025):** Creator Jon Erwin said the Goliath origin sequence was driven by generative AI tools (Midjourney, Magnific, Topaz, Runway, Kling) combined with traditional VFX. Reports put AI shots at about 72–73 of about 850 VFX shots (reported figures; I could not reach the primary Variety interview).
- **Time claim:** Runway's customer story quotes Erwin saying the sequence took "a couple weeks" rather than "four or five months", and lists "5 months" of post-production time saved ([Runway](https://runway.com/customers/how-house-of-david-used-runway-to-become-amazons-latest-hit-series)). **Vendor claim.**
- **Season 2:** 350–400 AI shots, which TheWrap describes as "5x more" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/house-of-david-season-2-showrunner-pro-ai-shots/)). Wired also reported "over 350" ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/amazons-house-of-david-used-over-350-ai-shots-in-season-2-its-creator-isnt-sorry); paywalled). Erwin's rationale was that he lacked the budget to realize the battle scenes practically. No dollar figures were published.

#### Amazon MGM Studios: AI Studio (2026)
- In February 2026 Reuters reported an internal AI Studio led by Albert Cheng to "lower production costs" and speed workflows, with a closed beta in March and "results by May" ([Idaho Business Review / Reuters, 2026-02-04](https://idahobusinessreview.com/2026/02/04/amazon-ai-movie-tv-production/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/amazon-launches-ai-studio-to-cut-film-and-tv-production-costs/)). **I found no published results.** Treat it as intent only.

#### Lionsgate and Runway (announced 2024-09)
- The deal promised a custom model trained on Lionsgate's catalog of more than 20,000 titles, for "capital efficient" content creation. A year later TheWrap reported that the library alone was insufficient to train a capable model, and that actors' ancillary rights complicated the work ([TheWrap, 2025-09-22](https://www.thewrap.com/lionsgate-runway-ai-deal-ip-model-concerns)).
- Vice Chair Michael Burns told Vulture he could convert a franchise into a PG-13 anime and "three hours later, I'll have the movie" ([Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/ideas-commentary/lionsgate-vice-chairman-michael-burns-claims-he-can-produce-anime-films-in-3-hours-with-ai-247611.html)). That is an aspirational remark, not an outcome.
- In June 2026 the partnership was restructured: Lionsgate took an equity interest in Runway and the two set up a joint development program ([Runway news](https://runway.com/news/runway-and-lionsgate-expand-partnership); [IBC](https://ibc.org/artificial-intelligence/news/lionsgate-takes-stake-in-ai-firm-runway/22794)). **No savings figures have been published.**

#### Lionsgate: *Fall* (2022) and Flawless
- The R-rated cut had more than 30 f-words. Flawless's TrueSync / DeepEditor visual dubbing altered the mouth movements for the replacement lines to reach PG-13. Director Scott Mann, who is also Flawless co-CEO, said the work took about two extra weeks, against reshoots that would have meant "multiple extra months and spending millions of extra dollars" ([SlashFilm, 2022-08-10](https://www.slashfilm.com/958424/the-fascinating-way-fall-transformed-its-r-rating-to-pg-13-using-deepfake-tech); [AV Club](https://www.avclub.com/deepfake-ai-remove-f-bombs-for-pg13-rating-fall-1849396029)).
- **Caveat:** The person making the claim co-runs the vendor. The baseline is hypothetical.

#### *Watch the Skies* (Sweden, 2025)
- The original cast re-recorded their lines in English and Flawless altered the lip movements. AMC screened it in May 2025 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/amc-theatres-will-screen-a-swedish-movie-visually-dubbed-with-the-help-of-ai-130022232.html)). Flawless reportedly covered the dubbing costs ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/311114/swedish-film-watch-skies-ai-visual-dubbing)). No cost comparison was published.

#### *Here* (2024) and Metaphysic Live
- On-set, real-time AI de-aging. Crew watched a live de-aged monitor feed ([THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/metaphysic-ai-tom-hanks-robin-wright-deaged-robert-zemeckis-caa-1235313318/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/105434-new-ai-powered-tech-allows-real-time-de.html)). Metaphysic claims it reduces post compositing ([Metaphysic](https://metaphysic.ai/live)). **No time or cost figures are public.** The value claimed is feasibility: the number of de-aging shots would have been impractical with traditional methods.

#### *The Brutalist* (2024): context, not savings
- Editor Dávid Jancsó said Respeecher was used to refine Hungarian vowel sounds ("mainly just replacing letters here and there"). This triggered an awards-season backlash, and director Brady Corbet said the performances were the actors' own ([THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/the-brutalist-ai-backlash-adrien-brody-1236113015/)). No savings were quantified. It is useful as a reputational-risk data point.

#### Tyler Perry pausing his $800M studio expansion (2024-02)
- Perry told THR the expansion (a dozen soundstages) was "currently and indefinitely on hold because of Sora" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/tyler-perry-studio-atlanta-expansion-open-ai-sora/); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/02/23/tyler-perry-halts-800m-film-studio-expansion-after-being-shocked-by-openais-sora)). This is an **anticipated** saving (avoided capex), not a realized production saving. I found no later update on whether the hold was lifted.

### Animation and "AI-native" studios

#### *Critterz* (Vertigo Films / Native Foreign / OpenAI)

## 4. Industry reports and studies

### Survey and measured data (what people reported)
- **CVL Economics, "Future Unscripted" (Animation Guild, Concept Art Association, Human Artistry Campaign; January 2024):** Surveyed 300 executives and managers between 2023-11-17 and 2023-12-22. About 75% said genAI supported eliminating, reducing or consolidating jobs at their companies ([report PDF](https://animationguild.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Future-Unscripted-The-Impact-of-Generative-Artificial-Intelligence-on-Entertainment-Industry-Jobs-pages.pdf); [THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/ai-hollywood-workers-job-cuts-1235811009/)). About 25% were using genAI and 47% planned to. The **projection** is about 203,800 US entertainment jobs "disrupted" within 3 years, including about 118,500 in film/TV/animation (21.4%) ([AWN](https://awn.com/news/animation-guild-shares-generative-ai-economic-impact-study-results)). This is a survey of executive expectations, not measured savings, and it was commissioned by labor and artist groups.
- **Deloitte survey data:** In Deloitte's 2024 enterprise survey, 42% of executives named efficiency, productivity or cost reduction as the top AI benefit (cited in [Deloitte TMT Predictions 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2025/tmt-predictions-hollywood-cautious-of-genai-adoption.html)).
- **Zhou & Lee, PNAS Nexus (March 2024):** More than 4 million artworks from more than 50,000 users on an art platform. Text-to-image adopters' output productivity rose about 25%, and average novelty declined ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10914360)). This is peer-reviewed and observational, but it measures **individual artists' output, not studio production cost**.

### Projections and models (not outcomes)
- **Roland Berger, "AI innovations for VFX and animation" (2025-02-26):** A cost-benefit model of AI rotoscoping by genre, in person-days: action 2,835 → 2,250 (about 20%), period 785 → about 480 (about 40%), sci-fi/fantasy 6,210 → about 2,160 (about 65%), horror 1,620 → about 750 (about 55%), romcom 165 → about 90 (about 45%). It rates computer-vision tasks (roto, object removal, upscaling) as offering the highest value relative to cost ([Roland Berger](https://www.rolandberger.com/en/Insights/Publications/AI-Innovations-for-VFX-and-animation.html)). **Modelled estimates, not trial results.**
- **Bain, "Tech in Content Production: Will AI Kill the Video Star?" (2023-09-28):** Scenario estimates mostly about virtual production plus AI: 5–10% on a $50M comedy, up to 20% on a $100M family film, and $30–40M on a $200M sci-fi sequel with production-day costs down up to two-thirds. The authors call their genAI estimates "conservative". Its one historical example is a sci-fi budget cut by more than 40% ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/tech-in-content-production-will-AI-kill-the-video-star/)). **Projection.**
- **Morgan Stanley (2025):** GenAI could cut costs about 10% across media and up to 30% in TV and film ([Morgan Stanley](https://morganstanley.com/insights/articles/ai-in-media-entertainment-benefits-and-risks); the page timed out for me, so this figure is via secondary summaries). **Projection.**
- **Deloitte TMT Predictions 2025 (2024-11-19):** Large studios would spend less than 3% of production budgets on genAI content tools in 2025, with about 7% of *operational* spend shifting to genAI (contracts, permitting, marketing, localization) ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/technology-media-and-telecom-predictions/2025/tmt-predictions-hollywood-cautious-of-genai-adoption.html)). **Projection.** Notably, it predicts that most early value lands in operations and localization, not in on-screen content.
- **McKinsey, "What AI could mean for film and TV production and the industry's future":** Exists ([McKinsey](https://mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/what-ai-could-mean-for-film-and-tv-production-and-the-industrys-future)), but the page timed out repeatedly, so **I could not verify its figures.** Do not quote numbers attributed to it without checking.

### Academic studies of time savings in VFX, animation or editing
- **I found no peer-reviewed controlled study** that measures time saved on rotoscoping, cleanup or editing in a real production. The closest are the LAIKA prototype figures (studio-reported), Foundry's SmartROTO research, which had a stated **goal** of saving about 25% of artist time ([Foundry](https://www.foundry.com/insights/machine-learning/smartroto-enabling-rotoscoping)), and the Roland Berger model. This is an evidence gap.

## 5. Patterns

**Where savings look most credible**
1. **Narrow, bounded post-production tasks.** Single VFX shots (El Eternauta; the House of David Goliath sequence; The American Experiment's 17 minutes), rotoscoping and masking (LAIKA, Roland Berger), background plates and crowd or set extensions. Here AI replaces a well-understood unit of work, so "x times faster" is at least meaningful, even if self-reported.
2. **Delivery and localization.** Dubbing, visual dubbing and subtitling have the most repeated percentages (Deepdub 60% time / 40% cost; ElevenLabs/Gaia 25% / 10%). The biggest real effect is making *uneconomic* localization possible (Prime Video's undubbed titles), not discounting existing dubs. Deloitte's prediction points the same way.
3. **Still imagery for marketing and publication.** Klarna and Unilever give company-stated dollar or percentage savings on image production with large volumes. Product imagery is repeatable, brand-controlled and tolerant of iteration.
4. **Fixes in post that avoid reshoots.** *Fall* (dialogue replacement) and de-aging (*Here*, *Happy Gilmore 2*). The value is avoiding cost or making something feasible. The figures are hypothetical counterfactuals.
5. **Pre-production and previs.** Netflix (*Billionaires' Bunker*) and Toei's storyboards and layouts. Usage is widely reported but almost never quantified.

**Where claims are mostly hype or unproven**
- **Whole-feature AI economics** (Critterz, Staircase, Utopai, Toonstar's "90% cheaper", Promise's and Moonvalley's 20–67% savings). These are all targets or pitches from parties raising money. No feature has published an actual cost report.
- **"$2,000 ads".** The Kalshi figure excludes creative labor. Coca-Cola's AI spot used about 100 people and more than 70,000 clips. The speed is real; the cost claims are partial.
- **Studio–vendor "custom model" deals.** Lionsgate–Runway did not deliver a model as promised and was restructured.
- **Music "savings".** Most evidence is about feasibility (restoration, voice recovery) or volume (Deezer uploads), and much of the volume is spam or fraud. There is no documented label-level production-cost reduction.
- **Agency holding-company figures.** These mix AI with offshoring and restructuring. Havas's 10–50% is the only production-specific range found, and it is very wide.

**Recurring caveats to carry into any skill docs**
- Baselines are almost always hypothetical ("would have taken months").
- Speed claims are more common and more plausible than cost claims. Headcount often doesn't fall (Coca-Cola).
- Most numbers are per-shot or per-task. Never extrapolate them to whole productions.
- Backlash is a real cost: Coca-Cola, Toys"R"Us, *The Brutalist*, Netflix Japan, Toei and Prime Video's anime dubs all drew it.
